# Fall-distance cutoff sensitivity

## Bottom line

The tail effect is similar and statistically significant at **2.5 m and 2.8 m**. At
3.0 m it is smaller and no longer significant by the primary HC3 test. That cutoff
also excludes 15 of 73 trials, so it is more vulnerable to selection bias.

| Cutoff | Included | Tail effect | 95% CI | HC3 p | Permutation p |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **2.5 m (primary)** | 72 | +0.157 | +0.062 to +0.252 | 0.0016 | 0.0008 |
| 2.8 m | 69 | +0.151 | +0.051 to +0.251 | 0.0038 | 0.0016 |
| 3.0 m | 58 | +0.100 | -0.016 to +0.215 | 0.0892 | 0.0454 |

Positive effects favor the tail.

## Interpretation

- Use **2.5 m** as the primary cutoff because it retains 72 of 73 trials.
- The close 2.5 m and 2.8 m estimates support reasonable cutoff robustness.
- Do not interpret the 3.0 m permutation p-value alone as confirmation: its HC3
  confidence interval includes zero and 21% of trials are excluded.
- Freeze the 2.5 m cutoff and coupled metric before the next experiment. These
  analyses remain exploratory because both were evaluated after viewing the data.
